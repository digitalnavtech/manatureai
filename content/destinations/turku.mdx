Turku sits on Finland's southwest coast at the mouth of the Aura River, and it wears its history differently than Helsinki — this was Finland's capital and largest city until the early 19th century, and its medieval castle and cathedral are correspondingly older and more substantial than anything in the current capital. It's also the natural jumping-off point for the Archipelago Sea, one of the densest island clusters on Earth.

## Best Time to Visit

**May through September** is Turku's best window, when the Aura riverside restaurants and boats are in full swing and the archipelago ferries and cycling routes are running. The city's Medieval Market in late June and other summer festivals add to the appeal. Outside summer, Turku is quieter but still worthwhile for the castle, cathedral and museums, which operate year-round.

## Things to Do in Turku

- **Turku Castle** — one of Finland's largest surviving medieval buildings, with centuries of history and an extensive museum inside.
- **Turku Cathedral** — the "mother church" of Finland's Lutheran tradition, dating back to the 13th century.
- **Aura riverside** — restaurant boats, cafés and a pleasant walking route connecting the castle to the cathedral and market square.
- **Turku Archipelago Trail** — a signposted driving/cycling route across islands connected by bridges and ferries, ideal for a half-day or multi-day extension.
- **Forum Marinum and maritime museum ships** — moored historic vessels open for exploring along the riverside.

## How to Get There

Direct trains from Helsinki run roughly hourly and take about two hours, arriving centrally near the river. Driving covers similar ground via the E18 motorway. Turku is also a major ferry port, with regular services to the Åland Islands and overnight connections to Stockholm — useful if you're combining Finland with a wider Baltic or Scandinavian trip.

## Where to Stay

Central Turku, near the river between the castle and the market square, is the most convenient base and puts most sights within walking distance. Staying slightly further out is rarely necessary given the city's compact size, though archipelago-focused visitors sometimes prefer island accommodation for a night or two.

## Food and Cafés

Turku's riverside restaurant boats are a signature experience in summer, ranging from casual to upscale. The city has a strong contemporary Finnish food scene alongside classic market-hall fare at the Turku Market Hall, one of the oldest and best-preserved in Finland. Archipelago-inspired seafood — smoked and grilled fish especially — features heavily on local menus.

## Suggested Itinerary

A solid plan for a first visit: **Day 1** — castle, cathedral, and a riverside walk with a boat-restaurant dinner. **Day 2** — a half-day or full-day drive or cycle along part of the Archipelago Trail, returning to Turku in the evening. Combine with Helsinki as bookends of a longer Finland trip, connected by the two-hour train.
