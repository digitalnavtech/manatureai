Finnish Lapland is where the postcard version of winter Finland actually happens: snow-laden pine forests, the possibility of the aurora overhead, and a scale of wilderness that's hard to grasp until you're standing in it. It's also a legitimate summer destination, when the midnight sun replaces the northern lights as the main event.

## Best Time to Visit

**Late September to late March** is aurora season, with the darkest, clearest nights in December and January offering the best contrast (though also the shortest daylight — down to a few hours near the winter solstice). **February and March** balance decent aurora odds with longer daylight and reliable snow for skiing and husky or reindeer safaris. **June and July** bring the midnight sun, hiking season on the fells, and a much quieter, greener Lapland that many first-time visitors don't expect.

## Things to Do in Finnish Lapland

- **Northern lights chasing** — guided tours head away from town light pollution on clear nights; several resorts also offer wake-up-call services if the aurora appears overnight.
- **Husky and reindeer safaris** — a genuinely Lapland activity, typically ranging from short taster rides to full-day expeditions.
- **Rovaniemi and Santa Claus Village** — sitting almost exactly on the Arctic Circle, this is Lapland's main gateway city and a popular family stop.
- **Ski resorts (Levi, Ylläs, Saariselkä, Ruka)** — well-developed downhill and cross-country skiing infrastructure, plus non-skiing activities like snowshoeing and ice fishing.
- **Fell hiking in summer** — Lapland's fells (low, rounded mountains) offer some of Finland's best multi-day trekking, particularly in Urho Kekkonen and Pallas-Yllästunturi National Parks.

## How to Get There

Direct flights connect Helsinki to Rovaniemi, Ivalo and Kittilä in around 1.5 hours, with more frequent service and additional direct European routes during the winter season. An overnight sleeper train (with cars for cars, if you want your own vehicle up north) runs from Helsinki to Rovaniemi and is a scenic, if slower, alternative. Once in Lapland, distances between towns are large, so plan transport — rental car or resort shuttles — as part of your itinerary rather than an afterthought.

## Where to Stay

Lapland's accommodation ranges from standard hotels in Rovaniemi to glass-roofed igloos and aurora cabins near the resort areas of Levi, Ylläs and Saariselkä. Glass-igloo stays are popular for aurora viewing but book out many months ahead for the December–February peak; standard cabins and hotels offer more availability and better value if aurora photography specifically isn't the priority.

## Food and Cafés

Lappish cuisine leans on reindeer, Arctic char, cloudberries and other foraged ingredients, with sautéed reindeer (*poronkäristys*) served with mashed potatoes and lingonberry as the signature dish. Resort towns have a mix of hotel restaurants and casual cafés; independent restaurants are more limited outside the main towns, so many multi-day itineraries include half-board accommodation.

## Suggested Itinerary

A first Lapland trip commonly looks like: fly into Rovaniemi or a resort airport, spend the first day settling in and trying a short activity (husky ride or snowshoeing), dedicate one or two evenings to aurora chasing, and use remaining days for a mix of guided excursions and independent exploring.
