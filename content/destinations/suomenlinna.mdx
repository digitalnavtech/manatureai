Suomenlinna is an 18th-century sea fortress spread across six linked islands just off Helsinki's coast, and it remains one of the best reasons to add an extra half-day to a Helsinki itinerary. It's a UNESCO World Heritage Site, a functioning neighborhood with around 800 residents, and — thanks to the short public ferry ride — one of the easiest "island escapes" in Northern Europe.

## Best Time to Visit

**May through September** is ideal for walking the ramparts, picnicking on the grassy fortifications, and enjoying the island's cafés with outdoor seating. Early morning or late afternoon ferries in summer help avoid the midday cruise-ship crowds. The ferry runs year-round, and a **winter visit** — quiet, cold, and often snow-covered — offers a very different but equally worthwhile atmosphere, particularly for photography.

## Things to Do in Suomenlinna

- **Walk the fortress walls and courtyards** — the main historical structures are free to explore and connect most of the inhabited islands.
- **Suomenlinna Museum** — a good first stop for context on the fortress's Swedish, Russian and Finnish history.
- **Vesikko Submarine** — a WWII-era Finnish submarine open for a walk-through, popular with families.
- **Picnicking on the grassy ramparts** — a favorite local pastime in summer; bring your own food or pick up supplies before boarding the ferry.
- **The Toy Museum and several small galleries** — smaller, quieter stops for a slower visit.

## How to Get There

The HSL ferry departs from Market Square (Kauppatori) in central Helsinki roughly every 20–40 minutes depending on the season, and the trip takes about 15 minutes. A standard Helsinki public transport ticket covers the ferry, so no separate ticket is needed if you already have an HSL day pass or single ticket. A seasonal water bus also runs in summer for a slightly different, more scenic route.

## Where to Stay

Almost all visitors see Suomenlinna as a half-day trip from Helsinki rather than an overnight stay, since accommodation on the island itself is very limited. Basing yourself anywhere in central Helsinki, particularly near Market Square or Katajanokka, keeps the ferry crossing short and convenient.

## Food and Cafés

The island has a small but decent selection of cafés and restaurants, including a working brewery, several seasonal cafés, and a bakery near the main ferry pier. Many visitors prefer to bring a picnic — a very Finnish way to spend an afternoon on the fortifications — and pick up coffee or ice cream locally to round it out.

## Suggested Itinerary

A well-paced half-day: take a mid-morning ferry over, walk the main fortress route and visit one or two museums, have lunch or a picnic on the ramparts, then catch an afternoon ferry back to Helsinki in time for dinner in the city. It combines naturally with a Helsinki city-centre morning or a Design District afternoon on either side.
