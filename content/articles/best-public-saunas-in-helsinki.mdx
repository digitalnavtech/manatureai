Public sauna culture isn't a tourist activity in Finland — it's a genuine, everyday part of local life, which is exactly what makes trying one worthwhile as a visitor. Helsinki has options ranging from architecturally striking waterfront complexes to a century-old neighborhood institution.

## Löyly

Löyly's angular wooden waterfront building is one of Helsinki's most photographed pieces of contemporary architecture, and the sauna experience matches the design ambition — multiple sauna rooms, a terrace, and direct access to the sea for a cold plunge between rounds. Booking ahead is strongly recommended, especially for evening slots.

## Allas Sea Pool

Located right on the harbour near Market Square, Allas combines sauna facilities with outdoor pools (including a heated one for winter use), making it a good option if you want to combine sauna time with swimming and a harbour view. Its central location makes it the easiest to fit into a single day of sightseeing.

## Kotiharjun Sauna

Helsinki's last continuously operating traditional wood-heated public sauna, tucked into a residential street in Kallio since the 1920s. It's unpretentious, inexpensive relative to the newer venues, and the closest thing to how Finns experienced public saunas for most of the 20th century. A good choice if you want authenticity over design polish.

## Sompasauna

A free, community-run, self-service sauna on the waterfront near Kalasatama, entirely volunteer-maintained and open to anyone. There's no staff and no fixed schedule for wood supply, so it can be hit-or-miss — but it's a genuinely local, off-the-radar option for visitors comfortable with a more informal setup.

## Sauna Etiquette for Visitors

- **Shower first** — always rinse off before entering the sauna itself, at every venue.
- **Swimwear rules vary** — mixed public saunas require it; some traditional single-gender sessions don't. Check each venue's policy.
- **Hydrate** — saunas run hot (commonly 80–100°C), and alternating with a cold plunge or shower is both traditional and genuinely helps you last longer.
- **Keep noise moderate** — saunas are generally treated as a place to unwind rather than socialize loudly.
- **Bring or rent a towel** — most venues offer rental if you didn't bring your own.
